Try it: choose the bottom nav style — Material 2 as it is today, Material 3's tonal pill, or a flat bar where the whole bar stays plain and the selected destination is marked by a primary line across its top edge. Tap the four destinations; on Loans use the single Filters control and the Record payment action on any card; on Transactions search or scan the day groups; in Settings the backend URL now sits under Advanced, Base Currency opens a picker, and Log out is in the danger colour.
Base currency: the picker offers the nine currencies the app supports, PKR first and selected by default. Choosing one converts the dashboard — the three tiles, the loans card, the category breakdown and top merchants all re-render in the new currency rather than just swapping the symbol. The Loans tab itself stays in PKR, because loans are tracked per person rather than converted at rest.
This round: the second tab is labelled Activity so it fits its slot at any phone width — the screen keeps its own Transactions title, so say the word if you would rather rename that too. Day headings are now computed from the real date, so Today and Yesterday only appear when they are genuinely those days and anything older is named outright (“Sun, Oct 11”). Every card carries a hairline edge in both looks, which is what stops the Loans, Expenses by Category and Top Merchants cards reading as washed out. Log out sits behind its own divider in the danger red, with room to scroll clear of the nav. And every lending figure now leads with what is left — Ali Raza shows Rs 18,000 left with the original Rs 30,000 demoted to the line beneath, and the By-person totals and the payment sheet agree with it. The scrollable tab bodies no longer show the browser's own scrollbar: it is a grey thumb on a white track in light and a mid-grey thumb on the app's background in dark, so it reads as part of the screen — open Settings and scroll to see it. The callout in the Record payment sheet was tied to the browser's colour scheme rather than the phone's, which is why its text could come out light blue on white and all but disappear; it now tracks the phone's own theme.
Transitions: every tile opens the same way — the income / expense / balance tiles, the dashboard cards, a By person row, a loan card and a transaction row all grow into their own detail out of the tile you touched, with the rows inside mirrored from that tile so the two can never disagree. The surface keeps its corner radius and colour through the first half of the grow, the content fades in 110 ms later over 150 ms, and back collapses it in 300 ms with the content leaving first. Sheets slide up from the bottom edge over a fading scrim and leave a little quicker than they arrive, so Record payment, the loan filters, the currency picker and the review prompt all animate instead of appearing. Switching tabs slides the incoming screen in from the side the tab order came from while the indicator line glides across, and the list follows in 40 ms steps, six items at most. Flip Motion to Reduced for the fallback: no slide, no stagger, no growth, just a short fade — which is also what the prototype does on its own if your system already asks for reduced motion.
Sign in: reachable from the chrome's Screen control or, the way the app reaches it, from Log out in Settings. It is pre-auth, so it replaces the whole tab shell rather than becoming a fifth destination. Three layouts to judge under Sign-in layout — Plain, Hero and Card. There is deliberately no "continue as" shortcut and no remembered account, so the form is the only place an account is ever named. Try signing in with nothing filled: the form shakes and reports each field inline, with a banner, rather than firing a snackbar over the top. With details that pass, the button shows its own spinner and then hands back to the dashboard. The two links open sheets, because a reset and a "here is where that lives" note are quick decisions, not screens.
Code-only, not visible here: predictive back on Android 14+, edge-to-edge system bars, haptic feedback on nav taps, the tablet NavigationRail that replaces the bottom bar above 600 dp, and the Compose build of these transitions (SharedTransitionLayout for tile to detail, AnimatedContent with a shared-axis slide for tabs).
